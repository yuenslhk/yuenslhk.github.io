Canon New F-1: A Professional Flagship Between Mechanics and Electronics

The New F-1 holds a pivotal place in the history of Canon SLRs. It was the last professional flagship of the manual-focus FD system, and the camera in which Canon struck its best balance between mechanical and electronic design. It appeared in 1981 and went on to serve countless news and sports photographers throughout the 1980s.

The F-1 heritage

In 1971 Canon launched the original F-1, its direct challenge to the Nikon F series in the professional market. The F-1 was known for its toughness and complete system, and an improved version, the F-1n, followed in 1976.

By the early 1980s, electronics were clearly the way forward and automatic exposure was becoming common. Canon needed a new flagship that kept the mechanical reliability professionals depended on while offering modern electronic features. The New F-1 was born from that need.

A hybrid shutter: keep shooting when the battery dies

The New F-1's most admired feature is its hybrid shutter. The fast speeds are mechanically controlled, so you can keep shooting at those speeds even with a dead battery. The slow speeds and automatic exposure are electronically controlled for greater precision.

For professional photographers this was a very practical design. In freezing conditions outdoors, in remote locations or on long assignments, a battery can fail at any moment. With a mechanical shutter as a back-up, the camera doesn't simply stop working. The shutter curtains are made of thin titanium foil, which gives them professional-grade durability and precision.

A modular system

The core idea behind the New F-1 is modularity. Photographers could swap components to configure the camera into exactly the tool they needed.

Interchangeable finders: The standard eye-level finder gives manual exposure. Fit the AE finder and the camera gains aperture-priority automatic exposure. There are also waist-level and high-magnification finders, suited to macro, copying and specialist work.

Motor drives and automatic exposure: With the AE power winder or AE motor drive fitted, the camera offers shutter-priority automatic exposure as well as continuous shooting. In other words, depending on the accessories fitted, the same New F-1 can be a manual camera, an aperture-priority camera or a shutter-priority camera.

Interchangeable focusing screens: The New F-1 offers a range of focusing screens. More unusually, changing the screen also changes the metering pattern, so photographers could choose average, partial or spot metering to suit the subject. Linking the metering mode to the focusing screen in this way was unique among cameras of its time.

Built for professional use

The New F-1 has a metal body and a robust construction that stands up to the demands of news, sports and outdoor photography. Canon put particular emphasis on reliability, and the body was rigorously tested to work dependably in extreme temperatures and under heavy use.

The camera soon became the main tool of many professionals and was a familiar sight at news events and major sporting competitions. Canon also produced a Los Angeles Olympics commemorative edition, underlining its standing in sports photography.

The FD lens system

The New F-1 uses the Canon FD mount and gives access to the large range of FD lenses, from ultra-wides and standards to super-telephotos. The L-series lenses use fluorite and special low-dispersion elements for outstanding image quality, and they were essential tools for news and sports photographers. With such a complete system of lenses and accessories, the New F-1 could handle almost any assignment.

The final chapter of the FD era

In 1987 Canon introduced the EOS autofocus system with the new EF mount, and the FD system was gradually replaced. The New F-1 stayed in production into the early 1990s as the last professional flagship of the FD system. Canon's later flagships, such as the EOS-1, belonged fully to the autofocus age.

The New F-1 is still sought after by many film enthusiasts and collectors on the second-hand market. Its tough body, flexible configuration and the reassurance of its mechanical shutter have made it shine again as film photography makes a comeback.

Conclusion

The New F-1 was born at the turning point between two eras. It embraced the convenience of electronics without giving up the reliability of a mechanical camera, and its modular design met the needs of very different photographers, showing how well Canon understood the professional market. As the last FD flagship, the New F-1 is more than a camera. It sums up Canon's manual-focus era.
